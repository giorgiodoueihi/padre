# Worked example

One full pass, showing the internal loop: patterns found, draft, remaining tells, final.

## Input

> I recently spent five unforgettable days in Lisbon, and let me tell you, this city completely stole my heart. Nestled along the banks of the Tagus River, Lisbon stands as a vibrant testament to Portugal's enduring spirit. Yes, the famous hills are challenging, but every climb rewards you with breathtaking, panoramic views. And the food? Simply divine. The pastéis de nata are a beloved national treasure.
>
> Would I go back? Absolutely. Lisbon isn't just a place to visit, it's a place to fall in love with, again and again. If you're dreaming of your next getaway, this is one destination that promises memories to last a lifetime. ✨

## Patterns found

Chatbot framing ("let me tell you"), promotional language (nestled, vibrant, breathtaking, divine), significance inflation (testament, enduring spirit), rhetorical question opener, negative parallelism, generic upbeat conclusion, emoji.

## Draft

> I spent five days in Lisbon last October and I am still a little split on it. The hills are not a detail. My hotel was in Alfama, which meant a long staircase every time I wanted coffee. The custard tarts were worth the hype. I would go back, but in spring and with better shoes.

## Remaining tells

- Too compressed: it drops the texture of the original instead of rewriting it.
- The closer is flat and generic.

## Final

> I spent five days in Lisbon last October and still have mixed feelings about it. Beautiful, yes. Also harder on the knees than anyone warned me.
>
> The hills never make the brochures. My hotel was up in Alfama, which photographs well and in practice means climbing a six-storey staircase every time you want coffee. By the second day my calves had opinions. The custard tarts, though, earn the fuss. I had one at a plain little place in Graça, still warm, and for about thirty seconds I understood why people build trips around pastry.
>
> I would go back, but in spring and with better shoes. Lisbon does not bend over backwards to make things easy for you. I think I liked that, even when my legs disagreed.

Note what changed: the rewrite kept the same coverage (hills, food, wanting to return), swapped adjectives for specific scenes, and let the mixed feelings stand.
