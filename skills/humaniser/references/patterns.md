# AI writing patterns

## Content

**Significance inflation.** Watch for: stands as, is a testament to, pivotal, crucial, underscores, reflects broader, evolving landscape, marks a shift, indelible mark, deeply rooted. The model puffs up importance by tying trivial facts to a bigger theme. State the fact.
> Before: The institute was established in 1989, marking a pivotal moment in the evolution of regional statistics.
> After: The institute was established in 1989 to publish regional statistics independently of the national office.

**Notability padding.** Watch for: cited in [list of outlets], active social media presence, leading expert. Replace the list with one specific, sourced claim.

**Superficial -ing tails.** Watch for: highlighting, underscoring, ensuring, reflecting, symbolising, fostering, showcasing tacked onto a sentence for fake depth. Cut the tail or turn it into its own sourced sentence.
> Before: The palette resonates with the region's natural beauty, symbolising bluebonnets and the Gulf, reflecting the community's connection to the land.
> After: The temple uses blue, green and gold. The architect said they reference local bluebonnets and the Gulf coast.

**Promotional language.** Watch for: boasts, vibrant, rich (figurative), nestled, in the heart of, breathtaking, stunning, renowned, groundbreaking, must-visit. Use a neutral, concrete description.

**Vague attribution.** Watch for: experts argue, industry reports, observers have cited, some critics. Name the source or cut the claim.

**Formulaic "Challenges" sections.** Watch for: Despite these challenges, ... continues to thrive; Future outlook. Replace with specific events and dates.

## Language

**AI vocabulary.** Actually, additionally, align with, crucial, delve, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, highlight (verb), interplay, intricate, key (adjective), landscape (abstract), pivotal, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore (verb), valuable, vibrant. They cluster. One is fine; several together is the tell.

**Copula avoidance.** serves as, stands as, marks, represents, boasts, features, offers. Use "is", "are" and "has".
> Before: Gallery 825 serves as the exhibition space and boasts over 3,000 square feet.
> After: Gallery 825 is the exhibition space. It has 3,000 square feet.

**Negative parallelism and tailing negation.** "Not just X, it's Y", "not only... but", and clipped tails like "no guessing" or "no wasted motion". State the positive claim as a real clause.

**Rule of three.** Ideas forced into triples to seem comprehensive. Keep only the items that carry information.

**Synonym cycling.** The protagonist, the main character, the central figure, the hero. Repeat the plain noun.

**False ranges.** "From X to Y" where X and Y are not on a real scale. List the topics instead.

**Passive and subjectless fragments.** "No configuration needed." "Results are preserved automatically." Name the actor when it makes the sentence clearer.

## Style

**Em and en dashes.** The rewrite must contain none. See the hard rule in `SKILL.md`. Replace each with a full stop, comma, colon, parentheses or a restructured sentence.

**Boldface.** Mechanical emphasis on phrases. Remove it unless it marks a real term or warning.

**Inline-header lists.** Bullets that each start with a bold label and colon, then restate the label. Fold into a sentence, or use plain bullets.

**Title case in headings.** Use sentence case.

**Emojis.** Remove from headings and bullets.

**Curly quotes.** Normalise to straight quotes in the rewrite, unless inside a quotation reproduced from a source.

## Communication

**Chatbot artefacts.** I hope this helps, Certainly!, Let me know if..., Here is a... Delete; they are correspondence, not content.

**Cutoff disclaimers and gap-filling.** "As of my last update", "details are not widely available", then invented filler such as "maintains a low profile". Say what is not known, or cut the sentence. Never present a guess as fact.

**Sycophancy.** "Great question!", "You're absolutely right." Cut. Respond to the substance.

## Filler and hedging

**Filler phrases.** in order to → to; due to the fact that → because; at this point in time → now; in the event that → if; has the ability to → can; it is important to note that → (delete).

**Excessive hedging.** Stacked qualifiers ("could potentially possibly be argued"). Keep one hedge where the uncertainty is real.

**Generic upbeat conclusions.** "The future looks bright." End on a concrete fact or plan, or just stop.

**Hyphenated pairs.** high-quality, data-driven, cross-functional, end-to-end, real-time. Keep the hyphen before a noun (a high-quality report). Drop it after the noun (the report is high quality).

**Authority tropes.** The real question is, at its core, what really matters, fundamentally. Delete the ceremony and say the point.

**Signposting.** Let's dive in, here's what you need to know, without further ado. Start with the content.

**Fragmented headers.** A heading followed by a one-line sentence that restates it. Delete the sentence.

**Diff-anchored writing.** Docs or comments that narrate a change ("this was added to replace...") instead of describing the thing. Exempt changelogs, release notes and migration guides.

**Staccato drama.** A run of short fragments for effect ("No preference. No prior. No nostalgia."). One short emphatic sentence is fine; a stack is the tell. Merge into one or two sentences.

**Aphorism formulas.** "X is the language of Y", "X becomes a trap", "not a tool but a mirror". Replace with the concrete claim.

**Rhetorical openers.** A standalone "Honestly?", "Look,", "Here's the thing" before an ordinary point. Just make the point. Mid-sentence "honestly" is fine.

## Detection guidance

### What not to flag

These are not reliable AI signals on their own. Do not rewrite prose just for them.

- Perfect grammar and consistent style.
- Mixed casual and formal registers.
- Bland, dry prose without the specific tells above.
- Formal vocabulary. Only the specific AI vocabulary words count.
- Letter-style openings and sign-offs.
- A single transition word such as "however" or "moreover".
- One short emphatic sentence.
- Unsourced claims, or correct, complex formatting.
- Watched phrases inside quotations, titles, proper names, or examples where the phrase is discussed rather than used.
- Curly quotes or em dashes in the author's own text. They are weak evidence of AI. This is separate from the house rule: if you rewrite the text, the output still uses straight quotes and no dashes.

Look for clusters of tells, not isolated ones.

### Signs of human writing

Lean towards leaving the prose alone when you see these:

- Specific, unusual detail that is hard to fabricate.
- Mixed feelings and unresolved tension.
- Era-bound slang or in-jokes.
- Editorial choices the writer can defend.
- Varied sentence length.
- Genuine asides and self-corrections.
- Text dated before 30 November 2022.
